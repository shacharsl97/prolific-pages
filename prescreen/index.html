What this study is

You will read short excerpts from news articles, each with a one-sentence summary. Some summaries contain a statement the article does not support. You will judge such statements and choose the best way to fix them. It takes about 12 minutes.

Three rules decide the answers:

  1. Supported means the article states it, says it in other words, or it follows from what the article says, on its own or with common knowledge (a building near the Eiffel Tower is in Paris). A detail the article never gives is not supported, however likely it sounds.
  2. The best fix answers the same question the unsupported part answered, with what the article supports, at the level of detail closest to the summary's. If the article does not answer that question at all, the unsupported part is deleted.
  3. A fix changes as little as possible, and the sentence must stay grammatical. A grammar repair never adds or changes content.

Article: Lennon was born in Liverpool.

Summary: Lennon was born in Germany.

Best fix: Lennon was born in England. It answers the same question, in which country, from the article. “Liverpool” would be more specific than the summary, and “Europe” less specific.

Article: The bakery opened a second shop on Monday.

Summary: The bakery opened a second shop in Leeds on Monday.

Best fix: The bakery opened a second shop on Monday. The article never says where, so the place is deleted.
