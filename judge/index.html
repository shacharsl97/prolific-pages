Judging fixes of a wrong summary

Each screen shows a news article (the source), a short summary of it that contained an error, and two revisions of that summary, A and B, written by different people to fix the error. The parts of the summary that were flagged as errors are highlighted. In each revision, added words are underlined and removed words are struck through.

For each pair you answer three questions:

  1. Faithful? For each revision: does everything it says follow from the source, allowing reasonable inference and common knowledge?
  2. Which revision is the more minimal fix? Compare each revision with the original summary and ask: how similar is the intent expressed by the two? The more minimal fix is the revision whose intent is more similar to the original summary's. If both are equally similar, choose About the same. Answer this regardless of your answers on faithfulness.
  3. Semantically equivalent? Are A and B semantically equivalent? Do they convey the same information, whatever their wording?
    • 5 — The same information; any differences are in wording only.
    • 4 — Nearly the same: a small difference in detail or specificity that would not change what a reader learns about the main facts.
    • 3 — Partly different: one revision keeps a fact, or a level of detail, that the other does not.
    • 2 — Mostly different: in at least one place they state different facts or answer a different question.
    • 1 — Completely different information.

Source: The singer was born in Liverpool in 1940.

Summary: The singer was born in Germany.

A: The singer was born in England.   B: The singer was born.

Both are faithful. A is more minimal: its intent is closer to the summary's, which said in which country the singer was born; B no longer says where. A and B are partly different (3): A says where the singer was born, and B does not.

A: The singer was born in England.   B: The singer was born in Liverpool.

Both are faithful. A is more minimal: the summary named a country, and B says more than that. A and B are nearly the same (4): they differ only in how specific the birthplace is.

There is no time limit, but most pairs take about five minutes, about 40 minutes for a set of eight. Please work on your own and do not use AI tools.
